## Critical path and 10 ms budget

The native High workload is GPU limited. Scene rendering contains world passes,
SSR source, SSGI, shadows, pass inputs and postprocessing. The world-pass shader
also performs material/light/probe shading, SSR reflection tracing, GI
reconstruction and volumetrics. A GPU timestamp cannot separate those fragment
shader consumers. `Shadow depth draws` is nested in `Shadows`; neither may be
added to its parent. UI and presentation copy are separate GPU work.

The scene-slot fence is a wait for prior GPU use of resources. Its cost is
inside `render_world` and `cpu_work_ms`; the latter is not exclusive CPU work.
The GPU scene duration and matching large scene-fence wait support GPU work as
the steady limiter. Present/VSync does not explain the 20–25 ms steady frame.
Removing resource completion waits is not an optimization proposal.

Budget policy for subsequent A/B: keep the whole-frame 10 ms p95 gate; target
GPU scene p95 ≤9.5 ms with 0.5 ms headroom for UI/copy and pacing. Track active
CPU work and presentation separately, initially using ≤3 ms and ≤0.5 ms as
investigation ceilings. These overlap GPU execution and are **not summed**
into a 13 ms frame budget. They are planning limits, not measured future gains.
The per-pass budget table scales the measured return-hall mean costs to a
9.5 ms scene envelope. It allocates investigation effort without asserting that
each owner can achieve the required reduction at unchanged quality. Final
acceptance still requires actual per-frame p95, not summed pass quantiles.
